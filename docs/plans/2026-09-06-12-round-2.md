### A1. `ND` is allowed for sex, and it makes body fat REQUIRED

The owner asked (r2.11) that the sex control be deselectable, defaulting to `ND` for
non-disclosed, so someone uncomfortable answering can pass. **Ruled: yes, and body fat becomes
required on that path.**

The reason it cannot simply be optional is that sex is read by three engines, and one of them
cannot run without it:

| Engine | With a sex | With `ND` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resting metabolic rate, Mifflin-St Jeor | `+5` male, `-161` female | **cannot compute** |
| Resting metabolic rate, Cunningham | not used unless body fat is given | **works: no sex term** |
| Beverage target, IOM 2005 | 3000 mL male, 2200 mL female | no sex-free figure exists |
| Body fat from tape, US Navy | different equation per sex | **cannot compute** |

Averaging the two Mifflin-St Jeor constants would invent a coefficient, which this project does
not do. So `ND` is workable only through the equation that has no sex term, and that equation
needs fat-free mass, which needs a body-fat percentage.

**This is the route the app already recommends.** `src/content/sexRationale.ts` tells the reader
in as many words: supply a body-fat percentage and the app switches to the equation with no sex
term. The rule makes the screen behave the way the explainer already promises.

**Three consequences, all of which must ship together or the state is incoherent:**

1. `ND` makes the body-fat field **required**, on the same step. The `Not measured` option is
   unavailable while sex is `ND`.
2. **`ND` disables the tape method.** The Navy equations are structurally different per sex, not
   differently weighted, so the tape route cannot produce a number without one. With `ND`, body
   fat comes from the percentage entry only, and the tape option says why it is unavailable.
3. **The beverage target shows a RANGE.** Ruled by the owner: with `ND` the screen shows 2200 to
   3000 mL and states that the reference intake is published per sex. It does not pick one
   silently and it does not average them.

### A2. A step's answers commit on Next, not on keystroke

> "the data of a field cell should not be replaced at the entry point (store it as temp), but it
> should be replaced only when 'NEXT' is pressed."

Adopted. It is what makes `ND` implementable as a default that Next overrides, and it makes Back
non-destructive.

**One tension to solve rather than discover.** Round 1's most valued feature was that setup
survives a closed browser (`AppState.setupDraft`, claim C1.G.1). If a field only reaches the
draft on Next, closing the browser mid-step loses everything typed on that step, which is exactly
the loss that feature exists to prevent.

**Resolution: persist BOTH tiers.** `setupDraft` keeps the committed answers; a second field
keeps the uncommitted buffer for the step in progress. Restoring returns the user to their step
with their half-typed values intact, and the committed answers stay untouched until they press
Next. Do not implement A2 without this, or a fix for one claim silently breaks another.

The peer session is scoped to asset generation only. It briefly took the round-2 intake and the

### A4. One entry per UTC offset is refused (r2.09)

He asked whether a conventional one-per-offset list exists. It does not, and it should not.
Measured against the platform's own 418 zones: 37 distinct offsets in January, and **16 of them
split into different offsets by July**. New York and Panama are both UTC-05:00 in January; in July
New York is -04:00 and Panama is not. Collapsing them breaks reminders for eight months of the
year, which is the defect `src/domain/export/fixtures/athens-dst-week.ics` exists to catch.

**What ships instead:** the list sorted by current offset then by name, which is the real defect
he reported, and `(UTC/GMT-05:00)` in the parentheses, which he asked for and which is reasonable.
Optionally, filtering aliases to canonical zones, which is an honest reduction; measure how many
it removes before promising it.
