Design G — pocket pet

A pixel pet on a segment LCD whose state is a direct function of the plan data — fed by completed sessions, drooping after a skip, asleep during a pause, and showing its needs attention screen exactly when the missed-week trigger fires. The rest timer is the same LCD counting down, because it is the only clock the device has. The mapping from WeeklyReview.delta and SessionAssignment.status to pet state is printed in full below the frames and in short on screen 3.

Trade dress. This borrows the 1990s virtual-pet genre. Bandai's Tamagotchi is that genre's canonical product; its name and its moulded egg-and-keychain shell are Bandai's marks and are not used. The device here is a plain rectangular handheld, the skin id is "pet", and no user-facing string carries a third-party mark.

Where the type is allowed to go. Press Start 2P on LCD numerals and four-word headings; Silkscreen on LCD chrome that is read and never tapped, 12 px floor; Work Sans for every button label and every line of prose, on the shell, outside the glass. The primary action is a docked 64 px bar, not a hardware button.

Pet state is a function, not a mood

Every state below is derived from types already in docs/plans/2026-09-01-00-master-plan.md §5. The skin adds no new state — it renames one. Note the last row in particular: markMotivationShown already sets WeeklyReview.missHandled = true, so "feeding the pet" and "dismissing the missed-week modal" are the same state transition.

Assets. Type: Press Start 2P, Silkscreen and Work Sans, Google Fonts, SIL Open Font License 1.1. Pet sprites and icon-strip glyphs: hand-authored 16 × 16 pixel grids rendered as inline SVG rects with shape-rendering: crispEdges — own work, no third-party rights. Kenney's 1-Bit Pack (CC0, no attribution required) was considered and not used: the pet needs seven states in one silhouette redrawn to a two-value palette clearing 6:1 on the LCD ground, and sourcing seven matching poses from a general tile pack and recolouring them is more work and less control than authoring the grid. Kenney remains the right source if this direction ships and needs a wider icon set.

Measured limitation. The always-on ghost icons sit at roughly 2.4:1 against the LCD ground and do not meet the 3:1 non-text contrast guideline. That is inherent — a ghost on a segment LCD is low-contrast by definition. They are therefore decorative and aria-hidden: every state they stand for is also printed as a word on the LCD and on the shell, and nothing depends on them alone. Lit icons render at full #0F380F.
