# Round two — four skins, planned before they were drawn

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Scope:** visual direction only. Nothing under `src/`, `legacy/`, `docs/plans/` or `docs/review/` is
touched. Deliverables live in `docs/design/round2/`, generated assets in `agy-artifacts/`, sources in
`REFERENCES.md`.
**Status of this file:** written in full before a single line of HTML, as required. Sections 1–7 are
the plan; §8 records the `agy` critique and what changed because of it; §9, §10 and §11 were
appended after the four mockups were built and verified.

Round one produced four directions and the user's verdict was: D (Coach board) and B (Clinical
dashboard) are good, A and C are dull, and the whole set is not creative enough. Round two answers
that, and it answers it with a structural idea rather than four more colour schemes.

## 1. The structural idea: these are *skins*, not redesigns

`docs/plans/2026-09-01-00-master-plan.md` §3 sets the tone rule for all user-facing copy: *"clinical,
formal, honest; no hype, no emoji, no motivational filler."* Two of the four directions below break
that rule deliberately, because the user asked for exactly that. The way to have both is to stop
treating tone as a property of the app and start treating it as a property of a **skin**.

A skin is two things and nothing else:

1. **A token set.** Colour, type, spacing, radius, motion. Already how round one worked.
2. **A copy table.** A `Record<CopyKey, string>` mapping every user-facing string key to that skin's
   phrasing. The clinical values are the default table; a skin overrides entries.

```ts
// proposed for a later plan; NOT added to master-plan §5 by this task
export type SkinId = "clinical" | "brat" | "stan" | "pet" | "board";
export type CopyKey =
  | "start_session" | "rest" | "skip_today" | "pause_plan" | "train_other"
  | "week_delta_negative" | "week_delta_zero" | "week_delta_positive"
  | "hydration_cue" | "pr_reached" | "motivation_title"
  | "session_cursor" | "plan_progress" | "add_30s" | "skip_rest" | "week_review_title";
export type CopyTable = Partial<Record<CopyKey, string>>;   // merged over the clinical default
// UiPrefs gains: skin: SkinId  (additive, Zod default "clinical", CURRENT_SCHEMA_VERSION stays 3)
```

Three rules make the skins safe, and every mockup below obeys them:

- **Numbers and units are never skinned.** `140 lb`, `63.50 kg`, `61.2 kg`, `1:47`, `2 of 4`,
  `+2.5 %` are produced by `src/domain/units.ts` and pass through the copy layer untouched. A skin
  may put a word next to a number; it may never restate the number in words.
- **Plan-altering semantics are never skinned.** "on hiatus" still holds `PlanCursor.nextSessionIndex`
  at 11; "not today satan" still advances it to 12 and asks for a `skipReason`. Every mockup states
  the real semantics in fine print in the skin's own voice.
- **Every screen renders the same components with the same data.** If a skin needs a screen the
  clinical default does not have, the skin is wrong.

The copy table is rendered on each mockup page as a two-column reference — **default → skin** — so
the swap is visible and implementable rather than implied.

### 1.1 Sample data (identical to round one, so the directions compare on form)

Plan of 12 weeks × 4 sessions = 48. Ten completed, one skipped ⇒ cursor at `nextSessionIndex = 11`
⇒ **session 12 of 48**; 12 mod 4 = 0 ⇒ label **Upper**. Availability Mon/Tue/Thu/Sat, weekly target 4.
Reviewed week 2026-08-24 → 08-30: 2 completed, 1 skipped, 1 not logged ⇒ **delta −2**. Today is
Tuesday 2026-09-01, 18:00 slot, 8 minutes out. Rest running at **1:47 of 2:00**.

Two progression cases, one per unit system, on every Train screen:

- **Barbell bench press (lb).** All sets reached 8 reps last session ⇒ load advances. 2.5 % of
  135 lb = 3.4 lb, rounded to the 5 lb barbell step ⇒ **140 lb** (stored 63.50 kg).
- **Lat pulldown (kg).** Smallest stack increment 5 kg = 11 % of 45 kg, above the 10 % ceiling ⇒
  load holds and the rep target extends to **10–14**. Stack marked in kg, logged as entered.

Week-review targets: **2 340 kcal**, **protein band 130–160 g**, **fluid 2 900 mL**. Body mass
**61.2 kg**. Hydration cue carries no volume, per the content review.

## 2. Direction E — Brat Summer

**Concept.** One flat lime field, one stretched narrow sans in black, everything lowercase, and a
single mascot; nothing else is allowed on the screen. The joke is that the app has stopped trying to
impress you, which is also, accidentally, the most legible thing it could do.

**World it borrows from.** The cheap club-flyer and burned-CD-sleeve register of the early 2000s:
one Pantone, one typeface, artwork that has clearly been resaved a few times too many.
**The one detail only that world has:** the display type is *resampled* — set once and scaled up, so
its edges are soft — while the 11 px chrome around it is razor sharp. Uniform sharpness would read as
a design system; the mismatch is the tell. Implemented as `filter: blur(0.4px)` applied only at
≥ 40 px, never to body copy.

| `--ink` | `#000000` | all display and body type on the ground | **10.91:1** on `--brat` |
| `--ink-mute` | `#454545` | fine print, set numbers, footnotes | **4.98:1** on `--brat` |

**Recorded prohibition:** white on `--brat` is **1.92:1** and fails at every size. There is no white
type anywhere on the lime ground; white exists only inside `--panel`. `#4F4F4F` was the first choice
for `--ink-mute` and was rejected at 4.26:1 — `#454545` is the darkest value that reads as grey

One family at two widths, because that is what a one-Pantone flyer would have had. `text-transform:

**Layout.** No cards, no borders, no radii, no shadows. Hierarchy is size and nothing else: the
session label at 76 px, the cursor line at 15 px, the prescription as a plain unruled list. The
primary action is a full-bleed black bar docked at the bottom; the three plan-altering controls sit
directly above it as underlined lowercase text links at 48 px tap height — deliberately *not*
buttons, because buttons are decoration and this skin has none. The mascot sits at the top-right of
Today at 96 px, and nowhere else.

**Motion — the focus pull.** On load, the display line animates `filter: blur(6px)` → `blur(0.4px)`
and `letter-spacing: 0.08em` → `-0.03em` over 420 ms, staggered 90 ms per line, three lines. Nothing
else on the page moves, ever. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the animation is removed and the
type renders at its final state.

that did not write to this machine, so no reference image is reproduced here.

is even slightly off, the screen stops reading as deliberate and starts reading as unfinished — and
the blur is a real legibility cost paid for a joke, on a screen someone is looking at between sets
with their glasses fogged.

## 3. Direction F — Stan Twitter ("gay and stupid", affectionate)

**Concept.** Camp, maximal and loud: hot pink on aubergine, a scrolling marquee, drop-shadowed display
type, inline emoji, glitter, and a rubber stamp that lands on a personal record. The register is a
group chat that is genuinely delighted for you and completely unserious about itself.

**World it borrows from.** Fandom stan accounts and the risograph gig-poster look they screenshot —
layers that do not respect each other's margins, four colours that should not be adjacent.
**The one detail only that world has:** the **quote-tweet**. One card on the Week review quotes the
app's own earlier statistic back at itself, with the new remark stacked above the quoted block. No
other visual culture produces that shape, and it happens to be a very good way to show a
week-over-week comparison: last week's number is literally embedded inside this week's comment.

### Palette

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--ground` | `#16041F` | page ground | — |
| `--surface` | `#260934` | cards, quote blocks | — |
| `--ink` | `#FFFFFF` | body type | **19.60:1** on ground · **17.93:1** on surface |
| `--ink-2` | `#C9A6DC` | secondary type, timestamps | **9.31:1** on ground · **8.51:1** on surface |
| `--pink` | `#FF5FCB` | primary action, stamp, hearts | **7.24:1** on ground · black on pink **7.24:1** |
| `--acid` | `#D6FF3B` | positive delta, sparkles, marquee | **17.01:1** on ground · black on acid **17.01:1** |

Six tokens exactly. `#5AE7FF` (13.36:1) was drafted as a fourth hue and cut: three saturated accents
on one ground is already at the limit of what stays readable, and the cyan was doing nothing pink and
acid were not. Every accent clears 4.5:1 as text *and* clears it inverted, so pink and acid can each
be either type or fill without a second look-up.

### Type

| Role | Family | Licence | Fallback stack |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display / stamps / marquee, 22–64 px | **Bungee** | SIL OFL 1.1 | `"Bungee","Impact","Haettenschweiler",system-ui,sans-serif` |
| Body + numerals, 12–34 px, `wght 400/600/800` | **Work Sans** (variable) | SIL OFL 1.1 | `"Work Sans",-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,"Segoe UI",Roboto,sans-serif` |

**Rubik Mono One considered and rejected:** it ships a single weight, and at 12–14 px — where most of
this screen actually lives — it is a monospace slab that resists reading. Bungee was designed for
signage and holds its shape at 22 px; Work Sans carries the small sizes with a real weight range and
tabular figures. Two families, two jobs.

**Layout.** Vertical stack of cards on the aubergine ground, each card rotated between −1.5° and
+1.5° so nothing lines up, with a 3 px offset drop shadow in `--pink` or `--acid`. A marquee runs
under the status bar carrying the plan cursor and the week's standings. The primary action is a
full-width pink slab with a 4 px black offset shadow. Plan-altering controls are three outlined
pills. Emoji are inline SVG at the type's optical size, never in a text run where they could be
substituted by the platform font.

**Motion — the stamp landing.** On the Train screen a personal record fires one 700 ms timeline:
the `MOTHER` stamp scales 2.4 → 1.0 and rotates −14° → −6° with a hard ease-out, and at 240 ms twelve
sparkle particles fan out on individual translate/rotate/fade tracks. The marquee runs continuously
at 22 s per loop. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the stamp renders in place at final scale,
the particles render static at 40 % opacity, and the marquee stops and becomes a centred static line.

someone spent ill or bereaved reads as mockery rather than affection, and no amount of pink recovers
it. The rule that keeps it safe is narrow and has to hold in every string: **the joke is always about
the app, never about the user** — which means this skin can never be the default, and probably has to
suppress its funniest line on the one screen (`motivation_title`) where a missed week is the subject.

## 4. Direction G — Tamagotchi coach

**Concept.** A pixel pet lives on a segment LCD set into the top of the screen, and its state is a
pure function of the plan data — fed by completed sessions, drooping after a skip, asleep during a
pause, and showing its "needs attention" screen exactly when the missed-week trigger fires. The rest
timer is the same LCD counting down, because that is the only clock the device has.

**World it borrows from.** The 1997 virtual-pet keychain: a moulded plastic shell, three hard
buttons, and a low-resolution grey-green LCD.
**The one detail only that world has:** the **always-on icon strip**. On a real segment LCD every
icon is printed permanently onto the glass and only some are energised, so the unlit ones sit there
as pale ghosts. The screen therefore shows you the *skip* and *attention* icons even when nothing is
wrong — you can see the whole vocabulary of things that could happen to you, dimmed. No modern UI
does that, and it turns out to be an honest one: the states are never hidden.

### Palette

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--shell` | `#241E2B` | moulded device body, page ground | — |
| `--shell-ink` | `#FFFFFF` | body type on the shell | **16.21:1** on shell |
| `--shell-ink-2` | `#B3A8C2` | secondary type, button labels | **7.18:1** on shell |
| `--lcd` | `#9BBC0F` | LCD ground | — |
| `--lcd-ink` | `#0F380F` | energised LCD pixels, all LCD type | **6.02:1** on `--lcd` |
| `--candy` | `#FF8FD0` | the one hardware accent: button rings, PR marker | **7.79:1** on shell |

**Recorded prohibition:** the classic DMG mid-tone `#306230` is **3.29:1** on `--lcd` and fails for
text at every size. It is used only as interior shading inside the pet sprite, which is a large
graphic, never for a character, a label or a numeral. `--lcd-dim` `#8BAC0F` (ghost icons) holds
`--lcd-ink` at **5.03:1**, so even the dimmed strip is readable.

### Type

| Role | Family | Licence | Fallback stack |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LCD numerals and headings, 20–56 px | **Press Start 2P** | SIL OFL 1.1 | `"Press Start 2P","Courier New",monospace` |
| LCD chrome labels, 8–12 px | **Silkscreen** (Regular + Bold) | SIL OFL 1.1 | `"Silkscreen","Press Start 2P",monospace` |
| Everything on the shell, 12–18 px | **Work Sans** | SIL OFL 1.1 | `"Work Sans",-apple-system,BlinkMacSystemFont,"Segoe UI",Roboto,sans-serif` |

Press Start 2P is unreadable as body copy and enormously wide per character — it earns its place on
numerals and four-word headings and nowhere else. That constraint is why the shell exists at all:
prose has to live somewhere, and it lives outside the glass in Work Sans.

**Layout.** The LCD is a fixed 358 × 268 inset panel at the top of every screen with a 6 px darker
bezel; below it sit three physical buttons (A / B / C, 56 px, `--candy` rings) and then the shell
content in ordinary type. The pet occupies the LCD's left two-thirds; the icon strip runs across the
top; a four-segment feed meter runs down the right edge, one segment per session in the weekly target.

### The pet-state mapping — a direct function of the data

This table is rendered on the mockup page, because a mascot whose mood is decorative is worthless and
one whose mood is derived is a chart.

| Data condition (types from master-plan §5) | Pet state | LCD shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a `PlanPause` with `to === null` exists | **ASLEEP** | pet lying down, `Zzz`, sleep icon lit, feed meter frozen |
| today's `SessionAssignment.status === "in-progress"` | **TRAINING** | pet mid-rep, two-frame cycle, timer icon lit |
| today's `SessionAssignment.status === "completed"` | **FED** | pet upright, feed meter +1 segment |
| most recent `SessionAssignment.status === "skipped"` | **DROOPING** | pet slumped, skip icon lit, meter unchanged |
| `WeeklyReview.delta === 0` | **HAPPY** | pet upright, all four meter segments |
| `WeeklyReview.delta > 0` | **THRIVING** | pet upright with the `--candy` marker |
| `WeeklyReview.delta <= -2` **and** `missHandled === false` | **NEEDS ATTENTION** | attention icon blinking at 1 Hz; the missed-week video is the care action that clears it |

Note what the last row does: `markMotivationShown` already sets `WeeklyReview.missHandled = true`, so
"feeding the pet" and "dismissing the missed-week modal" are the same state transition. The skin adds
no new state — it renames one.

**Motion — the idle cycle.** The pet has a two-frame idle at 900 ms per frame (a one-pixel vertical
bob, hard-cut, no interpolation, because a segment LCD cannot tween), and in NEEDS ATTENTION the
attention icon blinks at 1 Hz. That is the whole motion budget. Under `prefers-reduced-motion:
reduce` the pet holds frame A and the attention icon renders solid rather than blinking — the state
is still visible, it just does not flash.

honest" it is the direction that most needs a guardrail: the pet may never say anything the numbers
do not, and the numbers stay printed beside it.

## 5. Direction H — Departures board

**Concept.** The plan is a flight schedule: each session is a service with a label, a scheduled time
and a status, and the split-flap board resolves left to right when the screen loads. It keeps round
one's clinical register exactly and proves the same discipline can be witty without slang.

**World it borrows from.** The Solari split-flap board of a 1980s airport concourse.
**The one detail only that world has:** the **flap seam** — a hairline across the vertical middle of
every character cell where the two halves of the flap meet, and, because the cells are mechanically
identical and aligned, that seam is *continuous across the whole row*. It reads as one line spanning
the board rather than a per-character artefact, which is the single cue that says "mechanical" rather
than "LED".

### Palette

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--board` | `#0B0B0C` | board ground, page ground | — |
| `--flap` | `#17181A` | flap face | — |
| `--amber` | `#FFB000` | scheduled / on time / primary action | **10.74:1** on board · **9.70:1** on flap · black on amber **10.74:1** |
| `--paper` | `#E8E4DA` | departed services, body type | **13.99:1** on flap |
| `--mute` | `#7C8288` | held services, column headers, fine print | **5.06:1** on board |
| `--alert` | `#FF6B4A` | cancelled services | **6.98:1** on board |

Four statuses, four distinct values, all clearing 4.5:1 — and, importantly, distinguishable without
colour, because each status also prints its word (`ON TIME`, `DEPARTED`, `CANCELLED`, `HELD`). A
green `#5BD98A` (11.00:1) was drafted for `DEPARTED` and cut: a real board does not use green, and
`--paper` already separates a completed service from a scheduled one.

Monospace throughout, which is what makes the flap grid possible: every cell is one advance width, so
columns align without measurement. Tabular figures are automatic.

**Layout.** Every screen is a board. Today is a departures list — one row per upcoming session, with
today's service highlighted amber and marked `BOARDING`. Train is a gate screen: the rest timer is a
gate countdown in large flap cells, the exercise rows are a load sheet. Week review is the arrivals
summary — the reviewed week's four scheduled services with their outcomes, then the target rows as a
performance table. The primary action is a full-width amber bar reading `BOARD`.

**Motion — the flip.** On load each character cell cycles through six intermediate glyphs at 90 ms
each while a `rotateX` pseudo-element sweeps 0 → −90° and back, with a 40 ms stagger per column, so
the board resolves left to right in roughly 900 ms. It runs once. Under `prefers-reduced-motion:
reduce` the final characters render immediately with no flip and no glyph cycling.

## 8. The `agy` critique, and what changed because of it

One adversarial critique of §1–§7 was run through `agy` and is logged in `REFERENCES.md`. It is a
**paraphrase from another model**; nothing in it is treated as evidence, and it changed the plan only
where the change is independently defensible. It was asked six specific questions and told not to
summarise or compliment.

### Adopted

1. **Trademark exposure in direction G — adopted in full, and it is the most important change.** The
   critique's claim that the *name* and the moulded three-button keychain shell are Bandai's
   protected marks and trade dress, not a general retro aesthetic, is correct on its face and cheap
   to fix. Consequences: the direction is renamed **G — Pocket pet**, the file is
   `2026-09-01-design-G-pocket-pet.html`, no user-facing string and no `SkinId` contains the
   trademarked name, and the shell is redrawn as a plain rectangular handheld with a row of buttons —
   not an egg silhouette, not a keychain, not a lug. The genre is named once in this document as a
   *reference*, with the mark attributed to its owner, which is what a design document is for.
   The same test applied to E: "brat summer" is a descriptive cultural phrase and the mascot is an
   original drawing that names no artist, so the exposure is low — but the shipping `SkinId` is
   `"brat"` as a private token, and no lyric, album text or artist name appears in any file.

2. **Direction G's interaction model — adopted.** The critique is right that putting the primary
   interaction on three small A/B/C hardware buttons, and setting anything tappable in an 8 px pixel
   font, fails in a gym. Changes: the three buttons become a full-width row of 56 px controls
   **labelled in Work Sans**, not Silkscreen; the primary action moves out of the device metaphor
   entirely onto a docked 64 px bar; Silkscreen is restricted to LCD chrome that is *read and never
   tapped*, with a 12 px floor; and the prescription and set-logging rows live on the shell in
   16 px Work Sans, outside the glass. The LCD keeps the pet, the state word and the countdown —
   the three things worth looking at from two feet away — and gives up everything else.

3. **Direction E's tap targets — adopted.** Borderless 48 px text links are invisible affordances,
   and "invisible affordance" plus "sweaty thumb" is a miss. The three plan-altering controls keep
   the lowercase, unfilled, zero-radius look but gain a 2 px black outline. That is still the flyer
   idiom; a filled pill would not be.

4. **Blur on live numbers — adopted, and tightened beyond what was asked.** The resample effect is
   now restricted to the single 76 px session label on each screen. **No quantity is ever blurred** —
   not the rest clock, not a load, not a rep count, not a delta. A design device that degrades a
   number is not a design device, it is a data-integrity bug, and the earlier plan had it applied by
   font size alone, which would have caught the 88 px rest clock.

5. **Direction H needs its own geometry — adopted.** The observation that E and H are the closest
   pair, both being flat unruled vertical lists under a docked bar, is fair. H moves to a real
   four-column board grid — `TIME · SERVICE · STATUS · GATE` — with a persistent column header rule
   and right-aligned status column, which is a table, not a list. E moves the other way: no rows at
   all on Today, one oversized block per idea.

6. **Direction F's card rotation — adopted with a limit.** Rotation drops from ±1.5° to ±1.0°, and
   **no card carrying live set data or the rest clock is rotated at all**. Chaos is allowed to touch
   the chrome and never the numbers.

7. **The ghost icon strip — partially adopted.** The critique is right that a full dimmed vocabulary
   is clutter on a small panel. The strip is cut to four icons (sleep, skip, attention, timer)
   instead of the whole state set. The idea survives because it is the one detail that makes the
   panel read as a segment LCD rather than a small dark rectangle; the noise objection is answered by
   making it shorter, not by deleting it.

### Rejected

1. **"Cut direction E."** Rejected. The user asked for Brat Summer by name and gave the register in
   their own words. A critique that removes the requested item is answering a different brief. The
   legibility half of the objection is real and is adopted above as item 4.

2. **"Three of the four are structural clones, and the shared-component rule is an admission that
   they are cosmetic."** Rejected as framing, adopted as a specific fix. Shared structure is the
   deliverable: §1 rule 3 exists so that a skin is a token set plus a copy table and can therefore
   ship behind `UiPrefs.skin`. Four directions that each need their own component tree are four
   redesigns, which is what round one already delivered and what the user did not ask for again. The
   useful part of the point — that H and E were too close *within* that constraint — is item 5 above.

3. **Replacing `flop era (−2)` with `rebuilding era (−2)`.** Rejected as a silent edit. That string
   is the user's own authored copy, quoted verbatim in the brief, and it is aimed at a week rather
   than at a person; it appears directly beside the unskinned sentence "2 completed, 1 skipped, 1 not
   logged". The concern is legitimate enough to record rather than act on: **if the user wants a
   softer default, `rebuilding era (−2)` is the proposed swap, and `motivation_title` is the string
   to look at hardest** — "the intervention" heads the screen shown after a missed week, which is the
   one moment where the joke is closest to being about the person. The mockup keeps the user's word
   and makes the modal body plain and unjudging underneath it.

### Recommendation

<!-- decision: round2-skin-architecture | status: adopted | supersedes: none -->

**Adopt the skin architecture, and ship three of the four skins in this order: H, E, then F opt-in.
Reject G as a direction and take one idea out of it.** The architecture matters more than any single
direction here: `UiPrefs.skin` plus a `CopyTable` merged over a clinical default costs one token
module and one string map per skin, and it converts an argument about tone into a setting. Round one
recommended keeping the console shell and borrowing B's data layer; nothing here contradicts that,
because a skin sits *on* that shell rather than replacing it.

**H first, because it costs no tone concession.** It is the clinical register in different clothes,
so it needs no copy policy, no review, and no guardrail — it can be the default for anyone who finds
B too plain. It also composes with **B** better than anything in round one did: B's four distinct
encodings (sparkline for progression, categorical strip for compliance, bullet bar with a band for
the protein range, shared 1.25 × target scale) are exactly what H's *performance against target*
table wants, and H supplies the row grid and status vocabulary B's week review lacks. H is B in a
dark monospace register.

**E second, because it is the cheapest and it composes almost exactly with D**, the user's other
favourite. D is a near-black ground, one signal colour, display numerals and a stacked progress bar;
E is one flat ground and black display type. Swapping D's `--bg`/`--signal` for E's `--brat`/`--ink`
and lowercasing the copy table is a token swap plus a string map — D's stacked bar and its prominent
unit toggle survive untouched. E is also the only direction of the four whose legibility *improves*
on the clinical default, for the same reason B won that criterion in round one.

**F third, opt-in, never a default, and with one string re-reviewed.** It is the direction the user
asked for most specifically and it is the funniest thing in the set, but it carries the only real
downside risk. Two conditions: the rule "the joke is about the app, never the user" has to hold in
every string, and `motivation_title` — "the intervention", which heads the screen shown after a
missed week — is the one place where the joke is closest to being about the person. Keep the word,
make the modal body plain, and if the user wants a softer default, `week_delta_negative` →
"rebuilding era (−2)" is the swap `agy` proposed and I declined to make silently. F should be built
over **D's geometry**, not its own: D's 48 px floor, one filled action per screen and split rest tap
zones are the ergonomics F needs to stop being only decoration.

### Rejected alternatives

<!-- decision: round2-direction-pocket-pet | status: rejected | supersedes: none -->

**G — pocket pet, rejected as a direction; one idea taken.** It is the most inventive of the four and
its data-to-state mapping is the best engineering idea in the round: a mascot whose mood is a pure
function of `SessionAssignment.status` and `WeeklyReview.delta` is a chart, not decoration, and
"feeding the pet" and "dismissing the missed-week video" turn out to be the *same* transition
(`missHandled = true`) rather than two. It loses on three counts, in order of weight.

1. **It is the only one of the four that is not a skin.** It needs a new component — an LCD panel
   carrying a seven-state sprite, an icon strip and a feed meter — so it cannot ship as a token set
   plus a copy table. That breaks the architecture this whole round is built on, and it makes G more
   expensive than the other three combined.
2. **The ethical objection is not answerable by copy.** Making a skipped session look like a
   neglected animal is a stronger lever than a signed delta, and an app whose stated tone is
   "clinical, formal, honest" should not reach for a lever it cannot justify. I built the guardrail
   ("the pet never says anything the numbers do not") and I do not think the guardrail is enough,
   because the lever is in the picture rather than in the words.
3. **Trade-dress proximity is a standing cost.** The rename and the rectangular shell answer the
   objection today, but the better this direction gets, the closer it gets to Bandai's, and that is a
   bad gradient to be climbing.

**Taken from it:** the **feed meter** — four segments, one per session in the weekly target, filled
as sessions complete. It drops into D or B unchanged, and it is strictly better than a percentage for
the same reason D's stacked bar is: a percentage lets a miss disappear and a segment count does not.

<!-- decision: round2-copy-only-skin | status: rejected | supersedes: none -->

**A copy-only skin — clinical tokens, brat words — considered and rejected.** This is the cheapest
possible option and someone will propose it, so it is worth recording why it was not built. It tests
nothing. The user's objection to round one was that A and C were *dull to look at*; a skin that keeps
the clinical palette and swaps only the strings answers a complaint that was not made, and it would
have produced a fifth mockup indistinguishable from B in a screenshot. The token set has to move for
the question to be answered.
